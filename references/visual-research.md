## Reusable conclusions

1. Use a modular grid and a baseline rhythm; alignment should be visible even when grid lines are not.
2. Give every slide a dominant element occupying roughly 45–70% of visual attention.
3. Vary silhouettes across the deck: full-bleed media, asymmetric split, metric, quote, chart, and restrained text pages.
4. Keep one visual idea per slide. Avoid decorative shapes that do not reinforce the claim.
5. Treat photography and illustrations as layout anchors, not small thumbnails.
6. Mix bold and regular weights or serif and sans-serif intentionally; never mix fonts without a hierarchy job.
7. On charts, highlight the one series or datum that carries the conclusion and mute the rest.
8. Use one icon family per deck with consistent stroke, optical size, color, and background treatment.
9. Prefer fewer, larger elements to repeated UI cards.
10. Preserve consistency through grid, typography, and image treatment rather than repeating one layout.

Do not copy third-party slide compositions pixel-for-pixel or bundle their images. Rebuild only the general design principles above.
